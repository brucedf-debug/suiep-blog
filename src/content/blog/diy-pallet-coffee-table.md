A pallet coffee table is one of the most popular upcycling projects for good reason. The wood is often free, the build uses basic tools, and the rustic look suits many living rooms. What most tutorials skip is that a pallet straight off the loading dock isn't really coffee-table sized or coffee-table height, and not every pallet is safe to bring indoors. Plan for those three things and you'll end up with a table that looks intentional instead of improvised.

**Quick answer:** Choose a clean, heat-treated pallet stamped "HT." Cut it down to roughly 36–48 inches long and 20–24 inches deep, or use it whole in a large room. Raise it to about 16–18 inches with tall casters, legs or a second pallet. Sand thoroughly, add a plywood or glass top if you want a flat surface, and finish with stain or paint plus a durable clear coat. Expect a weekend, including drying time.

## Project Overview

| Detail | Estimate |
|---|---|
| **Difficulty** | Beginner to intermediate (cutting is the trickiest part) |
| **Active time** | 4–6 hours |
| **Total time** | 1–2 days including finish drying |
| **Rough cost** | Often $30–80 if the pallet is free; casters or legs and finish are the main expenses, and a glass top adds more |
| **Finished size** | About 36–48 in long, 20–24 in deep, 16–18 in tall |

## What You'll Need

**Materials**
- 1 clean heat-treated wood pallet (2 if you plan to stack them for height)
- 4 heavy-duty casters, 3–4 in or taller, or 4 table legs with mounting plates
- Wood screws (1¼–2½ in, depending on thickness)
- Scrap boards or 2x4 blocking to rebuild ends after cutting
- Wood filler (optional)
- Stain or [paint](/blog/how-to-paint-a-room/), plus water-based polyurethane or similar clear topcoat
- Optional: ½-in or ¾-in plywood, or tempered glass cut to size, for a flat top

**Tools**
- Circular saw or jigsaw, tape measure and square
- Drill/driver with bits
- Pry bar or claw hammer and a nail punch
- Orbital sander with 60, 80, 120 and 220 grit
- Dust mask (N95 or better), safety glasses, work gloves
- Brushes, rags or foam applicators

## Step 1: Choose a Safe Pallet

This decision matters more than any other. Look for the stamp, usually on the side of a corner block or stringer.

| Marking or condition | What it means | Use it? |
|---|---|---|
| **HT** (with IPPC wheat logo) | Heat-treated to kill pests | Yes, the best choice |
| **KD** | Kiln-dried | Generally fine |
| **MB** | Fumigated with methyl bromide | No |
| No stamp | Unknown treatment and history | Only if you know where it came from |
| Painted blue, red or brown pallets | Usually rental pallets owned by a pooling company | No, they aren't free to take |
| Dark stains, oily spots, chemical smell | Possible spills from past loads | No |

Always ask before taking pallets from behind a business. Some are returned for a deposit. Choose one with solid boards, few splits and no rot, and skip any that carried food that spoiled, chemicals or unknown liquids.

## Step 2: Plan the Size and Height

Standard North American pallets are about 48 × 40 inches, much deeper than a typical coffee table, and only about 5–6 inches tall. Two decisions shape the whole build:

**Footprint.** In most living rooms, a table about two-thirds the length of the sofa and 20–24 inches deep feels right. You can cut the pallet down, or use it whole as an oversized table in a large, open room.

**Height.** A comfortable coffee table is usually about the same height as the sofa seat, roughly 16–18 inches. Ways to get there:

| Method | Approximate height | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Single pallet + 3 in casters | 9–10 in | Very low; floor-cushion style |
| Single pallet + 5–6 in casters | 11–12 in | Lounge style, still below most sofas |
| Two stacked pallets + casters | 14–16 in | Sturdy, adds a storage level |
| Single pallet + 10–12 in legs | 16–18 in | Closest to a standard coffee table |

## Step 3: Clean and Inspect

Scrub the pallet with warm soapy water and a stiff brush, rinse, and let it dry completely, at least a day in the sun. Then look it over: pull protruding or bent nails, and replace boards that are split end to end or barely attached with scrap lumber of the same thickness. A coffee table takes more abuse than you'd think, from propped feet to heavy mugs.

## Step 4: Cut It Down (If Needed)

Mark your cut line so it falls just past a middle stringer or block, which gives you a solid edge to screw into. Check the cut path for hidden nails with a magnet or by eye, since hitting one can kick back or ruin a blade. Cut with a circular saw or jigsaw, clamping the pallet firmly. If the cut leaves an open edge, screw a scrap board or 2x4 across the ends of the top boards to close it up and restore strength.

## Step 5: Sand Everything

Pallet wood is rough by design. Wear a dust mask and sand outdoors if you can.

1. **60–80 grit** to remove splinters, stamps you don't want visible, and gray surface fibers.
2. **120 grit** to smooth the scratches left by the coarse paper.
3. **220 grit** on the top and edges, where hands, feet and drinks will land.

Round over sharp corners and edges slightly. Rushing this stage is the reason many pallet tables keep shedding splinters for months.

## Step 6: Add a Flat Top (Optional)

The gaps between pallet slats swallow pens, coasters and remotes. For a flat surface, you can:

- **Fill the gaps** with extra slats cut from a second pallet.
- **Screw on plywood** cut to size, then finish it to match or contrast with the frame.
- **Lay tempered glass** on clear bumper pads, which keeps the wood visible underneath.

## Step 7: Finish and Seal

Wipe off all dust. Apply stain for a warm, natural look, or paint for a modern one. Two thin coats beat one heavy coat; sand lightly with 220 grit between them once dry. Then add two or three coats of a clear protective topcoat, such as water-based polyurethane, especially on the top surface. Stain alone doesn't protect against spills and rings. Let it cure according to the product label, often several days before heavy use.

## Step 8: Attach Casters or Legs

Flip the table over and attach casters or leg plates to the solid corner blocks, not the thin slats. Pick screws long enough to bite deep into the block without poking through the top, and pre-drill to avoid splitting. Choose casters rated well above the table's weight plus whatever you'll set on it. If kids or pets might bump it, use locking casters on at least two corners. Check that all four sit level so the table doesn't rock.

## Adding Storage

If you stacked two pallets, the lower one becomes a shelf for books, baskets or blankets. A single pallet can get a thin shelf board screwed under the frame. The open ends of the pallet can also hold magazines, as long as the edges are well sanded. For more ideas on simple shelving builds, see our [floating shelf guide](/blog/diy-floating-shelf/).

## Troubleshooting

| Problem | Likely cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Table rocks | Uneven casters or a warped pallet | Shim under a caster plate or sand the high block |
| Splinters keep appearing | Sanding too quick or skipped grits | Resand with 120 then 220; add another topcoat |
| Stain looks blotchy | Mixed wood species and grain in pallet boards | Use a pre-stain conditioner or switch to paint |
| Top gets water rings | No clear topcoat | Sand lightly and add 2–3 coats of polyurethane |
| Screws won't hold | Screwing into slats or split blocks | Move fasteners to solid blocks; add blocking |
| Lingering smell | Residue in the wood | Clean again and air it out; if a chemical smell remains, don't use it indoors |

## FAQ

### Are pallets safe for indoor furniture?

Heat-treated (HT) pallets from a known, clean source are generally considered fine once cleaned and sealed. Avoid MB-stamped pallets, unstamped pallets of unknown origin, and anything stained, oily or smelly.

### How tall should a coffee table be?

About the height of your sofa seat, usually 16–18 inches, is comfortable for reaching drinks and books. Lower tables work for a lounge look but are harder to use from a standard sofa.

### Should I stain or paint a pallet table?

Stain shows off the wood's character but can look uneven because pallets mix wood types. Paint hides that and suits modern rooms. Either way, add a clear topcoat for durability.

### Can I build it without a saw?

Yes, if you use the pallet whole. Skipping the cut makes a large table, so it works best in a spacious room.
