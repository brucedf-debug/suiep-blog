A running toilet is more than an annoying sound. A constant leak can waste a surprising amount of water every day, and a slow one can go unnoticed for months while the water bill creeps up. Almost every case comes down to a few inexpensive parts inside the tank, and you can usually find the culprit in a few minutes just by watching a flush.

**Quick answer:** Take the tank lid off and look. If the water level is above the top of the overflow tube, lower the float on the fill valve. If the water is below it but still running or refilling on its own, the flapper isn't sealing: adjust the chain, clean the seat, or replace the flapper. If the fill valve hisses or won't shut off after adjusting the float, replace the fill valve. Confirm a flapper leak with a few drops of food coloring in the tank.

## What You'll Need

| Item | When you need it |
|---|---|
| Towel | Always; set the lid on it and catch drips |
| Food coloring or a dye tablet | To confirm a flapper leak |
| Replacement flapper | Worn, warped or stiff flapper |
| Replacement fill valve | Fill valve won't shut off or keeps hissing |
| Sponge and bucket | To empty the tank before replacing parts |
| Adjustable pliers | Loosening supply line nuts |
| Scouring pad | Cleaning the flush valve seat |

## Step 1: Diagnose by Watching

Lift the lid and set it on a towel; tank lids break easily. Flush and watch the full cycle: the flapper lifts, the tank empties, the flapper drops, and the fill valve refills the tank until the float shuts it off.

| What you see or hear | Most likely cause | Go to |
|---|---|---|
| Water flowing into the overflow tube | Water level set too high | Cause 1 |
| Tank refills by itself every so often ("phantom flush") | Flapper leaking slowly | Cause 2 |
| Constant trickle into the bowl, water below overflow | Flapper not sealing | Cause 2 |
| Handle must be jiggled to stop running | Chain too long, tangled, or handle sticking | Cause 3 |
| Fill valve hisses and never fully shuts off | Worn fill valve | Cause 4 |
| Water on the floor around the base | Not a running toilet issue; wax ring or crack | When to call a plumber |

## The Dye Test

To confirm a slow flapper leak, add a few drops of food coloring to the tank water and wait 15–30 minutes without flushing. If color appears in the bowl, water is leaking past the flapper. Flush afterward so the dye doesn't stain.

## Cause 1: Water Level Too High

If water rises above the overflow tube, it drains continuously into the bowl. Adjust the float so the water stops about an inch below the top of the overflow tube, or at the water line mark on the tank.

- **Cylinder (cup) float:** squeeze the clip or turn the adjustment screw on the fill valve to slide the float down.
- **Ball float on an arm:** turn the adjustment screw where the arm meets the valve, or gently bend a metal arm downward.

Make small changes, flush and check. A small adjustment moves the water line more than you expect.

## Cause 2: The Flapper Isn't Sealing

The flapper is the rubber seal at the bottom of the tank. Over time it hardens, warps or collects mineral deposits, and water seeps past it. In-tank bleach or cleaning tablets can speed up that wear.

1. Turn off the water at the supply valve behind the toilet and flush to empty the tank.
2. Feel the flapper. If it's stiff, warped or slimy and breaks down to the touch, replace it.
3. Wipe the flush valve seat (the ring the flapper sits on) with a scouring pad to remove mineral buildup that can stop a good seal.
4. Unhook the old flapper from the ears on the overflow tube and the chain from the handle arm.
5. Install a matching flapper: most are 2 inch or 3 inch. Check the size of the drain opening or take the old one to the store.
6. Turn the water back on and test.

## Cause 3: The Chain or Handle

The chain should have a little slack, about ½ inch, when the flapper is closed. Too tight and the flapper can't seal; too long and it can get trapped under the flapper. Adjust by moving the clip to a different link, and trim extra chain. If the handle itself sticks in the down position, tighten the nut inside the tank (it's usually reverse-threaded) or replace the handle.

## Cause 4: A Worn Fill Valve

If the float is set correctly but the fill valve keeps hissing or refilling, the valve's seal is worn. Some valves have a replaceable cap seal, but replacing the whole valve is often easier.

1. Shut off the water, flush and sponge out the remaining water.
2. Put a towel under the tank, then disconnect the supply line under the tank.
3. Unscrew the plastic locknut under the tank and lift out the old valve.
4. Set the new valve's height per its instructions, usually so the critical level mark sits at least an inch above the overflow tube.
5. Install, hand-tighten the locknut plus a small turn with pliers, reconnect the supply line and clip the refill tube to the overflow tube.
6. Turn on the water slowly, check for leaks and set the water level.

Universal fill valves fit most toilets, but some models use proprietary parts. If the valve is unusual, note the toilet brand and model from inside the tank.

## Less Common Causes

- **Cracked overflow tube:** water drains through the crack. The overflow tube is part of the flush valve, and replacing it usually means removing the tank. That's a bigger job, and many people call a plumber for it.
- **Refill tube too deep:** if the small refill tube is pushed down into the overflow tube below the water line, it can siphon water continuously. Clip it so its end sits above the water.
- **Canister-style flush valves:** some toilets use a tall cylinder instead of a flapper; a worn seal at its base causes leaks. Replacement seals are specific to the brand.

## After the Fix

Turn the water back on, flush once and listen for a minute after the tank refills. Repeat the dye test the next day to confirm a slow leak is gone. A quiet house at night is the best time to listen for faint refills.

## When to Call a Plumber

- Water on the floor around the base, which can mean a failed wax ring or a cracked bowl or tank
- A cracked tank or overflow tube
- A supply valve that won't turn off or leaks at the stem
- Leaks at the tank-to-bowl bolts that don't stop with gentle tightening

## FAQ

### How much water does a running toilet waste?

It depends on the leak. A small, silent flapper leak wastes much less than water pouring into the overflow tube, but either can add up over weeks.

### Why does my toilet run for a few seconds every so often?

That's a "phantom flush," usually a slow flapper leak that lowers the water level until the fill valve tops it up. Replace or clean the flapper.

### Do I need to turn off the water to adjust the float?

No, you can adjust the float with water on. Turn it off before replacing the flapper or fill valve.

### Are in-tank cleaning tablets bad for toilets?

Many plumbers advise against bleach-based tablets because they can wear out rubber parts like flappers faster. Check the toilet manufacturer's guidance.
