Falls from ladders are a common cause of serious home injuries, and many of them come down to using the wrong ladder: too short, rated too light or made of the wrong material for the job. The good news is that choosing well isn't complicated. Once you know what reach you need, how much weight the ladder carries and whether you'll work near electricity, the right choice usually becomes obvious.

**Quick answer:** Use a step ladder for indoor jobs and anything in open space, and an extension ladder for rooflines, gutters on two-story homes and high siding. Size it so you never stand on the top two steps of a step ladder or the top three rungs of an extension ladder. Choose a duty rating that covers your body weight plus tools, and pick fiberglass for any work near wiring or power lines.

## The Main Ladder Types

| Type | How it works | Best for | Limitations |
|---|---|---|---|
| Step ladder | Self-supporting A-frame | Ceilings, lights, painting, cabinets, low gutters | Limited height; must be fully opened and locked |
| Extension ladder | Leans on a wall; sections slide out | Roof access, two-story gutters, siding, trees | Needs a stable wall and firm, level ground |
| Multi-position ladder | Hinges into step, extension or scaffold shapes | Small storage spaces, varied jobs, stairways | Heavier; locking hinges need care |
| Step stool / platform ladder | Short, with a wide top platform | Shelves, closets, quick reaches | Low reach only |
| Telescoping ladder | Compact rungs that collapse | Tight storage | Must follow locking instructions exactly |

For most households, a 6-foot step ladder plus either a multi-position ladder or an extension ladder covers nearly every job.

## How to Size a Ladder

A ladder's labeled length is not the height you can reach. Work backward from the highest point you need to touch.

**Step ladders:** As a rough guide, the highest safe reach is about 4 feet above the ladder's labeled height for an average-height person, because you don't stand on the top cap or the step below it.

| Step ladder size | Typical max reach | Good for |
|---|---|---|
| 4 ft | About 8 ft | 8-ft ceilings, cabinets |
| 6 ft | About 10 ft | Most indoor ceilings, low exterior work |
| 8 ft | About 12 ft | Vaulted ceilings, first-floor gutters |
| 10–12 ft | About 14–16 ft | High ceilings, low rooflines |

**Extension ladders:** Choose one that, when set up at the correct angle, still reaches at least 3 feet above the edge you're climbing onto, like a roofline. Remember that sections overlap, so a 24-foot extension ladder doesn't give you 24 feet of usable length, and the angle reduces vertical height further. Manufacturers usually print a maximum working length and reach on the label; use those rather than the ladder's nominal size.

## Duty Ratings: Count Everything You Carry

Every ladder has a maximum load rating that includes your body weight, clothing, tools and materials. A gallon of paint, a drill and a pouch of fasteners add up quickly.

| Rating (ANSI type) | Maximum load | Typical use |
|---|---|---|
| Type III | 200 lb | Light household use |
| Type II | 225 lb | Medium duty, general DIY |
| Type I | 250 lb | Heavy duty, contractor grade |
| Type IA | 300 lb | Extra heavy duty |
| Type IAA | 375 lb | Special duty |

When in doubt, move up a rating. Higher-rated ladders are usually stiffer, and a ladder that flexes underfoot makes precise work like [painting](/blog/how-to-paint-a-room/) trim harder and less comfortable.

## Material: Aluminum, Fiberglass or Wood

| Material | Pros | Cons | Choose it when |
|---|---|---|---|
| Aluminum | Light, affordable, weather-resistant | Conducts electricity | You never work near wiring or power lines |
| Fiberglass | Non-conductive, sturdy | Heavier, costs more; can weather in sun | Any electrical work, outdoor work near service lines |
| Wood | Rigid, non-conductive when clean and dry | Heavy; needs inspection for rot and cracks | Rarely, mostly older or inherited ladders |

Never use an aluminum ladder near overhead power lines, and keep any ladder well clear of them. A wet or dirty fiberglass or wood ladder can also conduct electricity, so non-conductive doesn't mean risk-free.

## Features Worth Paying For

- **Slip-resistant feet** that swivel to sit flat on the ground.
- **Wide, flat or D-shaped rungs,** which are more comfortable during long jobs than round rungs.
- **Tool tray or paint shelf** on step ladders, used only as a tray, never as a step.
- **Leg levelers** for uneven ground or stairs.
- **Standoff (stabilizer)** for extension ladders, which holds the ladder away from gutters and widens its contact with the wall.

## Setting Up Safely

1. **Inspect before climbing:** rungs secure, rails straight, locks engaging fully, feet intact.
2. **Open step ladders fully** and lock the spreaders. Never lean a folded step ladder against a wall unless the manufacturer allows it.
3. **Set extension ladders at the right angle:** about 1 foot out from the wall for every 4 feet of height up to the support point (roughly 75 degrees).
4. **Check the ground:** firm, level and dry. On soft soil, use leg levelers or a sturdy base made for ladders.
5. **Extend 3 feet above the roofline** when stepping off onto a roof, and secure the top if possible.
6. **Keep three points of contact** while climbing, and face the ladder.
7. **Keep your belt buckle between the rails.** If you need to reach farther, climb down and move the ladder.
8. **Skip it in wind or rain,** and have someone steady the base of an extension ladder when possible.

These basics matter most on jobs like [cleaning gutters](/blog/how-to-clean-gutters-safely/), where you're reaching and moving debris at height.

## Storage and Care

Measure your storage space before buying: a full-length extension ladder needs a long wall or ceiling run. Store ladders off the ground on wall hooks or a ceiling rack, out of direct sun if they're fiberglass, and away from moisture if wood. Retire any ladder with bent rails, cracked rungs, missing feet or locks that don't engage. Don't try to repair a structural part.

## FAQ

### What size ladder do I need for an 8-foot ceiling?

A 4-foot step ladder reaches roughly 8 feet for most adults, which works for changing bulbs and painting. A 6-foot step ladder gives more comfortable working room.

### What size extension ladder do I need for a two-story house?

That depends on the height to the gutter or roof edge. Measure that height, add 3 feet if you'll step onto the roof, and check the manufacturer's maximum reach at the proper angle. Many two-story homes need a ladder in the 24–28 foot range, but measuring is the only reliable answer.

### Is a multi-position ladder as safe as a regular ladder?

Yes, when used exactly as the manufacturer describes and when every hinge lock is fully engaged. Check the locks each time you change positions.

### Can I stand on the top step?

No. The top cap and the step just below it on a step ladder are not for standing. Most ladders are labeled with the highest standing level.
