Clogged gutters rarely announce themselves until the damage is done: stained fascia boards, water pooling against the foundation, or ice dams along the roofline in January. Fall fills gutters faster than any other season, so a thorough cleaning after the main leaf drop keeps water moving away from your house instead of over the edge and down the siding. The job itself is simple. What matters most is doing it from a safe ladder position.

**Quick answer:** Clean gutters after most leaves have fallen, on a dry day. Set an extension ladder on firm, level ground at a 4-to-1 angle, extend it 3 feet above the gutter, and use a stabilizer so it doesn't rest on the gutter. Scoop debris in short sections, flush with a hose toward the downspout, and clear any downspout clog. Check slope, hangers and downspout extensions. Hire a pro for steep roofs, two-story gutters without safe ladder placement, or if you're uncomfortable at height.

## Why Gutters Matter

| Problem from clogged gutters | What happens |
|---|---|
| Overflow at the eaves | Rots fascia and soffits, stains siding |
| Water dumped near the foundation | Wet basements, erosion, cracks over time |
| Ice dams in winter | Water backs up under shingles |
| Heavy, soggy debris | Gutters sag and pull away from the house |
| Standing water | Mosquitoes, moss and plant growth |

## When to Clean

| Situation | Typical schedule |
|---|---|
| Few or no trees nearby | Once or twice a year |
| Deciduous trees overhanging | Late spring and late fall, plus a check mid-fall |
| Pines and other needle trees | Several checks a year; needles fall steadily |
| Gutter guards installed | Inspect yearly; clean guard surfaces as needed |

Aim for a dry day after most leaves are down. Dry debris is lighter and easier to scoop than a soggy mat.

## What You'll Need

- Extension [ladder](/blog/how-to-choose-a-ladder/) tall enough to extend 3 feet above the gutter
- Ladder stabilizer (standoff) so the ladder rests on the roof or wall, not the gutter
- Thick, waterproof gloves: debris hides sharp nails and metal edges
- Safety glasses
- Plastic gutter scoop or small trowel
- Bucket with a hook, or a tarp spread below
- Garden hose with a spray nozzle
- Plumber's snake for stubborn downspout clogs
- Slip-resistant shoes

## Step 1: Set Up the Ladder Correctly

Most ladder falls come from poor setup rather than the climb itself.

1. **Inspect the ladder:** rungs, rails, locks and feet in good condition.
2. **Choose firm, level ground.** On soft soil, use a ladder leveler or a solid base made for ladders. Never set up on a slope without leveling.
3. **Use the 4-to-1 rule:** for every 4 feet of height to the support point, move the base 1 foot out.
4. **Extend 3 feet above the gutter** so you have something to hold.
5. **Attach a stabilizer** to keep the ladder off the gutter and widen its contact.
6. **Keep three points of contact** while climbing, and keep your belt buckle between the rails.
7. **Move the ladder often.** Never lean sideways to reach farther.
8. **Stay clear of power lines,** including the service drop to your house, and have someone steady the base when possible.

## Step 2: Scoop the Debris

Work in short sections within easy reach. Start near a downspout and work away from it, so you don't push debris into the opening. Drop debris into a hooked bucket or onto a tarp below. Wet leaf sludge makes excellent compost; our guide to [composting at home](/blog/how-to-compost-at-home/) explains how to use it.

Pay extra attention to the area around the downspout opening, where debris compacts into the worst clogs.

## Step 3: Flush the Gutters

Once a run is scooped, spray water from the far end toward the downspout. Watch how it flows: water should move steadily toward the outlet without pooling. Rinse the remaining grit out.

## Step 4: Clear the Downspouts

If water backs up at the outlet, the downspout is clogged.

- Spray water down the spout from the top at full pressure.
- If it doesn't clear, feed a plumber's snake from the top or bottom.
- Tap along the downspout to find the clog; debris often catches at elbows.
- For stubborn clogs, disconnect the bottom elbow and clear it from below.

At the bottom, make sure extensions or splash blocks carry water at least a few feet away from the foundation.

## Step 5: Check the Gutter System

| Check | Sign of a problem | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Slope | Water stands in sections after flushing | Adjust hangers so gutters slope toward the downspout |
| Hangers and spikes | Sagging, gaps, loose fasteners | Replace with hidden hangers and screws |
| Seams and end caps | Drips at joints | Clean and reseal with gutter sealant |
| Fascia board | Soft, stained or rotted wood | Repair before reattaching gutters |
| Downspout extensions | Water pooling near the foundation | Reposition or add extensions |
| Roof edge | Missing drip edge, shingle grit piling up | Note it; a heavy grit load can mean aging shingles |

Take photos of anything unusual. It makes describing problems to a pro much easier.

## Gutter Guards: Worth It?

| Type | Pros | Cons |
|---|---|---|
| Mesh or micro-mesh | Blocks most debris, including needles | Fine grit and pollen can clog the surface |
| Perforated covers | Inexpensive, easy to install | Small debris still gets in |
| Reverse-curve (surface tension) | Sheds leaves well | Can overshoot water in heavy rain; visible from the ground |
| Foam or brush inserts | Easy DIY install | Collect debris and need periodic removal |

Guards reduce how often you clean, but they don't eliminate maintenance. Inspect them at least once a year.

## Ground-Level Options

For single-story homes, telescoping gutter wands and hose attachments let you clear gutters from the ground. They work best on light, dry debris and are slower on heavy, wet buildup. Some [leaf blowers](/blog/leaf-blower-buying-guide/) offer gutter attachments, but working with a blower on a ladder is risky; keep both feet on the ground if you use one.

## When to Hire a Pro

- Two-story or higher gutters without a safe, level ladder spot
- Steep roofs, or gutters above decks, stairs or slopes
- Power lines near the gutter line
- You're uncomfortable at height or working alone
- Repairs needed: sagging runs, rotted fascia or leaking seams

A professional cleaning costs far less than recovering from a fall.

## FAQ

### How often should gutters be cleaned?

Most homes need cleaning at least twice a year, in late spring and late fall. Homes with overhanging trees, especially pines, may need more frequent checks.

### Can I clean gutters from the roof?

Walking on a roof adds fall risk and can damage shingles. Working from a properly set ladder is safer for most homeowners.

### What's the best time of day to clean gutters?

A dry, calm day, after the morning dew has dried. Avoid wind and wet conditions, which make both the ladder and the roof edge slippery.

### Do I need to clean gutters with guards?

Yes, less often. Check guards yearly and clear debris from their surface and from any spots where fine grit has built up inside.
