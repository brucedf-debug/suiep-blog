Most first-time painters start with the walls because that's the part they care about, then end up with roller splatter on the ceiling or drips on freshly painted trim. Painting a room is mostly about order and preparation. Get those right and even a beginner can get clean lines and an even finish in a weekend.

**Quick answer:** Prep first: clear the room, clean and patch the walls, and tape where needed. Then paint top to bottom: ceiling first, walls second, trim and doors last. Cut in edges with a brush, then roll while the cut-in edge is still wet. Use two thin coats, prime when changing colors dramatically or covering stains and patches, and wait for the paint to cure before scrubbing or pushing furniture back.

## Project Overview

| Detail | Estimate |
|---|---|
| **Difficulty** | Beginner |
| **Time** | Typically 1–2 days for an average bedroom including drying between coats |
| **Paint needed** | Often 2 gallons of wall paint for two coats in a 12 × 12 ft room |
| **Main costs** | Paint, primer, roller covers, brushes, tape |

## What You'll Need

- Drop cloths (canvas is less slippery than plastic)
- Painter's tape
- Spackle, putty knife and fine sandpaper (see [patching drywall](/blog/how-to-patch-drywall/))
- 2–2½ in angled sash brush for cutting in
- 9 in roller frame, extension pole and roller covers (⅜ in nap for smooth walls, ½ in for light texture)
- Paint tray or 5-gallon bucket with a roller grid
- Primer, plus ceiling, wall and trim paint
- A sturdy [step ladder](/blog/how-to-choose-a-ladder/)
- Screwdriver for outlet covers, rags, a bucket of water

## Choose the Right Sheen

| Sheen | Look | Durability | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Flat / matte | No shine, hides flaws | Least washable | Ceilings, low-traffic bedrooms |
| Eggshell | Soft, low sheen | Wipeable | Living rooms, bedrooms, hallways |
| Satin | Gentle sheen | Scrubbable | Kids' rooms, kitchens, bathrooms, hallways |
| Semi-gloss | Noticeable shine | Very durable, easy to clean | Trim, doors, cabinets |
| Gloss | High shine | Hardest wearing | Doors, trim, furniture; shows every flaw |

Higher sheen is easier to clean but highlights dents and patches, which is why trim gets careful prep.

## Estimate How Much Paint You Need

A gallon usually covers about 350–400 square feet per coat on smooth, primed walls; check the can, since products vary. New drywall, fresh patches and textured walls absorb more.

1. Add up the length of all walls and multiply by the ceiling height.
2. Subtract about 20 sq ft per door and 15 sq ft per window.
3. Divide by the coverage on the can, then multiply by the number of coats.
4. Round up. A little extra is useful for touch-ups later.

Example: a 12 × 12 ft room with 8 ft ceilings has 384 sq ft of wall. Minus one door and one window leaves about 350 sq ft. Two coats at 350–400 sq ft per gallon is about 2 gallons.

## The Order: Ceiling, Walls, Trim

1. **Ceiling first.** Drips and roller spatter land on walls and floors you haven't painted yet.
2. **Walls second.** A little wall paint on the trim edge doesn't matter, because the trim comes later.
3. **Trim and doors last.** Glossier trim paint shows flaws most, so you don't want wall splatter on it afterward.

Some professionals paint trim before walls, because it's easier to tape off trim than to cut a crisp line along it. Both approaches work. The top-to-bottom principle is the part that matters.

## Step 1: Prep the Room

- Move furniture to the center and cover it. Remove curtains, wall hangings, outlet covers and switch plates.
- Lay drop cloths along every wall.
- Wash greasy or dirty walls (kitchens, hallways, around switches) with mild detergent and rinse. Paint doesn't bond well to grime.
- Fill nail holes and dents with spackle, let it dry and sand smooth.
- Spot-prime patches and stains so they don't show through as dull spots ("flashing").
- Caulk gaps between trim and wall with paintable caulk.

## Step 2: Tape Where It Helps

Tape along trim, ceiling lines and adjacent surfaces you won't paint. Press the edge firmly with a putty knife or your fingernail; a loose edge is why paint bleeds under tape. Confident painters often skip tape and cut in freehand with a good angled brush.

## Step 3: Paint the Ceiling

Cut in a 2–3 inch band around the edges with a brush, then roll the ceiling in sections, working across the shorter width of the room. Keep a "wet edge" by rolling into paint that's still wet. Use flat ceiling paint; it hides imperfections and reduces glare.

## Step 4: Paint the Walls

Cut in along the ceiling line, corners, outlets and trim on one wall, then immediately roll that wall so the brushed edge and rolled paint blend. Load the roller evenly, roll in a loose "W" or "N" pattern about 3 ft wide, then fill in with straight, overlapping vertical strokes from top to bottom. Don't press hard or keep reworking paint that has started to dry; that causes lap marks. Let the first coat dry per the can's recoat time, then apply the second coat.

## Step 5: Paint the Trim and Doors

Once the walls are dry, tape the wall side of the trim if you want extra protection. Use a smaller angled brush and paint baseboards, window casings and doors with long, smooth strokes along the grain. Two thin coats look better than one heavy one. Pull tape at a low angle while the last coat is still slightly soft, or score the edge with a utility knife if it has dried, to avoid peeling.

## Step 6: Let It Cure

Most latex paint is dry to the touch in an hour or two and ready to recoat in a few hours, but it can take days to weeks to fully cure. Wait at least 24 hours before rehanging pictures or pushing furniture against walls, and avoid scrubbing for a couple of weeks.

## Troubleshooting

| Problem | Likely cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Lap marks or stripes | Rolling over partly dried paint | Keep a wet edge; work one wall at a time |
| Paint bled under tape | Tape not pressed or wrong type | Touch up with a small brush; press edges firmly next time |
| Patches show as dull spots | Unprimed spackle | Spot-prime and repaint the wall |
| Old color showing through | Too few coats or big color change | Prime, then two coats |
| Drips and sags | Too much paint on brush or roller | Brush out while wet; sand and repaint once dry |
| Fuzz or lint in the finish | Cheap roller cover | Use a quality cover; roll it with tape to remove lint first |
| Peeling from tape removal | Pulled tape after paint fully dried | Score the edge first, then pull |

## Clean Up

For water-based paint, rinse brushes and rollers in warm water until it runs clear, working paint out of the base of the bristles. Oil-based paint needs the solvent listed on the can. For short breaks, wrap brushes and rollers tightly in plastic to keep them wet. Store leftover paint tightly sealed, labeled with the room, and keep it where it won't freeze. Dispose of paint according to local rules.

## Tips That Make a Big Difference

- **Box your paint:** mix multiple cans of the same color in a bucket so color is consistent across walls.
- **Ventilate:** open windows and run a fan; airflow helps paint dry evenly.
- **Watch humidity and temperature:** follow the can's minimum temperature, and expect slow drying in humid weather.
- **Use good tools:** a quality brush and roller cover matter more than most people expect.

## FAQ

### Do I need primer before painting?

Not always. Use primer on new drywall, patches, stains, glossy surfaces and big color changes. Many "paint and primer" products work well over already-painted walls in good condition.

### How long should I wait between coats?

Follow the recoat time on the can, often a few hours for latex. Cool or humid conditions extend it.

### Should I paint the ceiling the same color as the walls?

It's a style choice. White ceilings make rooms feel taller and brighter; matching colors can make a space feel cozier.

### Can I paint over glossy trim?

Yes, after cleaning, sanding lightly to dull the surface and using a bonding primer. Otherwise new paint may peel.
